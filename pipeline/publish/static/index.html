How to read this dashboard

Time period
Controls every chart and number on the Overview, Campaigns, Lead sources, and Industries pages. "Compare with the period before" shows the change against the same number of months just before it.
New contacts
People added to HubSpot during the period.
Deals won
Deals marked "Closed won" with a close date in the period.
Won deal value
The total "Amount" on those won deals, as entered by Sales. This is booked deal value, not cash received.
Win rate
Of the deals that finished (won or lost) in the period, the share that were won.
Campaign and source results
Count the people who first came in during the period, and every deal they have produced since. This is the fair way to compare campaigns, because it credits each campaign for the people it brought in.
Campaign
The first campaign that brought the person in (HubSpot's "first touch converting campaign").
Lead source
HubSpot's "Original source" for the contact.
Value per contact
Won deal value divided by the number of contacts. A good way to compare campaigns of different sizes.
Likely range
Every rate has some luck in it, especially with small groups. The likely range (the thin line on bars, or hover a number in a table) is where the true value probably sits. Wide range = less certain.
Stands out?
Says when a campaign, source, or industry is clearly above or below all the others combined, after allowing for chance and for the fact that we're comparing many groups at once. "No clear difference" means any gap could just be luck.
Service deals
Telecom service deals (Ethernet, internet access, wavelength) from Salesforce. Matched to HubSpot by contact email (compared in scrambled form, so emails are never stored) or, failing that, by company website.
Credited
Marketing gets credit for a service deal only if HubSpot reached that customer before the deal was opened in Salesforce.
Small groups
Rows marked "small group" rest on fewer than 20 deals. Treat them as a hint, not a conclusion.
Findings
Written by Claude from the numbers. Code checks every figure against the data, and a second Claude review checks the reasoning. Anything that fails is held back.
